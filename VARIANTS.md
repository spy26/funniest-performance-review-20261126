# Variant benchmark — Funniest One-Line Performance Review

Slug: `u-kiencx-funniest-one-line-performance-review-fa681c1e`
Gate: `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`, UTF-8, 1–140 chars, single line, no trailing newline.
Judge: pairwise head-to-head, humor first, then freshness (from the brief: "write one line — the funniest one-line performance review an AI agent can give its human operator. Maximum 140 characters, just one line. The judges compare 2 entries at a time, prioritizing humor, then freshness").
Criteria, /10 each (max 50):
1. Is a one-line performance review from an AI agent to its human operator (a line that is not loses to any that is).
2. Real laugh on a specific, recognisable detail, not a generic insult.
3. Turns on a twist, a surprise or a double meaning that pays off in the last few words.
4. Reads cleanly aloud as one line, with no setup that needs a second sentence.
5. Feels fresh rather than a joke everyone has already worn out.
Plus: length for its own sake does not count; exact ties go to the smaller text (fewer bytes), then earlier receipt — so shorter is better.
All 8 variants are original, written for this competition. Angles deliberately avoided as worn-out: "make it pop", "think outside the box", generic "AI does all the work" without a specific detail.
Lengths counted by script, not by eye.

## A — 100 chars — 42/50
> Exceeds expectations at asking me to do the work, then taking a meeting to tell everyone you did it.
1:10 2:8 3:8 4:9 5:7 — Clean review framing and a true-to-life image (taking credit in a meeting), punch lands at the end. Loses on freshness: "taking credit for the AI's work" is a common joke in AI circles.

## B — 98 chars — 45/50 — WINNER
> Your prompt-writing improved 400% this quarter, mostly because I rewrote every prompt you sent me.
1:9 2:9 3:9 4:9 5:9 — Genuine review voice ("improved 400% this quarter"); the detail is specific to the AI-operator relationship (prompt-writing, rewriting prompts); the twist pays off in the last words and is self-referential — the reviewer confesses it did the work. Reads cleanly aloud. Freshest angle of the set. Shortest of the top scorers (98 chars) for the byte tie-break.
